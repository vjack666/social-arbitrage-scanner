# Medline Inc. (Nasdaq: MDLN)

**Priority:** High  
**Snapshot date:** 2026-11-18  
**Research status:** Active monitoring  
**Classification:** Publicly listed company; this is not a pre-IPO case.

## What the company does

Medline is a medical-surgical products manufacturer, brand owner, distributor, and healthcare supply-chain services provider. Its customers include hospitals, surgery centers, physician offices, nursing homes, and other care providers. It offers roughly 335,000 products, including surgical/procedure kits, gloves and protective apparel, wound care, urological and incontinence care, and laboratory/diagnostic consumables. Its logistics footprint includes 70 distribution facilities and a large owned delivery fleet.

It reports two segments:
1. **Medline Brand** — products manufactured in its own facilities or sourced from global partners and sold under Medline's brand.
2. **Supply Chain Solutions** — distribution of third-party healthcare products plus customized logistics, procurement, and supply-chain optimization services.

The business is mission-critical and recurring, but distribution is lower-margin than branded products. This distinction matters when revenue grows faster than profits.

## Latest material developments

### 1. Secondary offering priced — confirmed, 2026-11-17
Medline announced that selling stockholders affiliated with Blackstone, Carlyle, Hellman & Friedman, GIC, and ADIA priced a secondary offering of **60 million shares at $35.50 per share**. Underwriters have a 30-day option for up to 9 million additional shares. The offering was expected to close on **2026-11-23**, subject to customary conditions.

**Important interpretation:** Medline itself is not selling these shares and will not receive the proceeds. The transaction can increase available float and create near-term supply/overhang, but it is not a capital raise for the company. Medline also agreed to repurchase $200 million of equity interests from selling holders in a separate private transaction, conditioned on the offering closing.

### 2. Q3 and first nine months 2026 results — confirmed
For the quarter ended 2026-09-26:
- Net sales: **$7.9B**, +10.4% year over year.
- Net income: **$318M**, down 1.2% year over year.
- Adjusted EBITDA: **$1.0B**, +11.8% year over year.

For the first nine months of 2026:
- Net sales: **$22.893B**, +10.9%.
- Net income: **$696M**.
- Adjusted EBITDA: **$2.8B**, with 12.2% adjusted EBITDA margin.

Segment detail for nine months:
- Medline Brand sales: $10.642B (+6.5%); segment adjusted EBITDA $2.775B (+9.2%).
- Supply Chain Solutions sales: $12.251B (+15.1%); segment adjusted EBITDA $575M (-2.2%). Segment margin fell from 5.5% to 4.7%.

**Interpretation:** demand and sales growth are strong, especially in Supply Chain Solutions, but mix and operating costs are pressuring that segment's profitability. Branded products contribute a disproportionate share of segment EBITDA. Tariff refunds influenced reported margin comparisons and should not be treated as a permanent structural tailwind.

## Social-arbitrage hypotheses to test

1. **Secondary-offer overhang:** does price/volume weaken around the $35.50 offering, and does it recover after closing?
2. **Fundamental divergence:** does the market reward double-digit sales growth while overlooking lower net income and margin pressure?
3. **Segment mix:** is faster growth in lower-margin distribution reducing consolidated earnings quality?
4. **Supply-chain resilience:** monitor public disclosures on logistics disruption, tariffs, inventory, and the 2026 Tracy, California distribution-center fire and related insurance recoveries.
5. **Ownership/float:** track follow-on filings, insider/sponsor sales, repurchases, and share-count changes.

These are hypotheses, not conclusions or trade signals.

## Risks / invalidation conditions

- Growth is not converting into earnings or free cash flow.
- Supply Chain Solutions margins continue deteriorating.
- Tariffs, inventory inflation, freight, or supply disruptions offset volume growth.
- Offering-related selling pressure persists beyond the expected transaction window.
- A market/healthcare-sector move explains the share-price change better than company-specific information.
- Public evidence is too sparse to establish a predictive social signal.
